This partial-result contribution concerns the fixed fourteen ac-r2-v1 moves on ordered freely reduced pairs in F(x,y), with one unit of cost per move. It develops entry criteria and scoped path obstructions around the already submitted 42-move connection from A=(xyxYXY,xxxYYYY) to S=(XyyyxYYYY,xxYYYYYXyyyyy). It does not give a path from S to a trivializable exit and does not prove AK(3) trivial.

For R_n=X y^n x Y^(n+1), let D_n=(R_n,xY^n) and E_n=(R_n,xxY^(n+1)Xy^(n+1)), n>=0. We retain the previously submitted n+8 construction for D_n and give an explicit 3n+12-move E_n construction, ending at the strict ordered pair(x,y), with construction peak 4n+10. The E_n endpoint and move count are formalized for every natural n including 0; its all-prefix peak calculation is a hand proof. A unimodular ordered pair with exactly one signed occurrence of either generator in the cyclic reduction of either relator reaches the standard pair and every D_n. The formal connection interfaces characterize unbounded reachability from A to each D_n or E_n by the existence of an S-prefix reaching such an entry hit. They supply no successful prefix and preserve no cost or cap bound. In particular S=(R_3,B_4) is neither E_3 nor E_4.

For the fixed S-to-E_0=(Y,xxYXy) problem, with arbitrary finite nonmix gaps and no intermediate word-length cap, we establish two scoped statements. Exactly four mixes require at least 24 atomic moves. Any path from S to E_0 with exactly six mixes must have one of sixteen residual row layouts. Across these layouts, 156 normalized signed matrix profiles have product I and 16 have product -I. In the -I case both original row inversion counts are odd, so a path of at most 23 moves has at most 15 single-letter conjugations. This is a necessary-condition classification, not a complete six-mix exclusion or an exclusion of every path of at most 23 moves.

The four-mix proof combines an oriented common-word reduction, the original-path bound N<=L+10 with N even, a Cayley-tree shortening to two B15 witness balls, complete exact scans, and eight finite dual certificates excluding arbitrary-length conjugators in four explicit group images.
